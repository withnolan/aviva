# 03: Concepts (creative-director)

Three different launches for **aviva**: one plain white A4 sheet, 80 gsm, launched as an AI product with ORYZO's craft and none of ORYZO's content.

Read with: `work/00-mission.md` (KEEP / CHANGE), `work/decisions.md` (#7–#10), `work/02-reference-teardown.md` (§2 scroll map, §7 copy patterns, §8 signature list + collision watch), `work/01-research.md` (facts; section numbers cited as `R§`).

Range: **A is elegant and dry. B is bold and absurd. C is unexpected.**

---

## Ground rules all three concepts follow

- **One sheet, one canvas, one scene.** The hero is always the same A4 sheet in an endless white studio (no desk, no props unless stated). Light palette: paper white, graphite and one ink-blue accent (the visual-designer decides the final values). Where ORYZO used photography, we use live renders of our own sheet or our own line illustrations.
- **Every concept uses all seven paper verbs**: bend, curl, fold, fly, crumple, tear, write. Each concept lists where each one happens.
- **Loader (decision #10):** a print-shop technical drawing: crop marks, 210 mm and 297 mm dimension lines and a numeric counter. No circles, no bezier handles, no dashed double outline, no conic fill, no flat green ground. The drawing turns into the sheet **once**, at the start, and never again mid-page.
- **No ORYZO signatures (teardown §8, items 1–54):** no fold-to-encrypt or typed text appearing on the object (#10, #36); no blank-PDF "weights" (#50); no power, uptime or "0 W" claims (#14, #40); no "Pro Max", glowing pills or red glow (#15, #47, #48); no frosted left panel, no serif formula ornament, no backronyms (#33, #35, #54); no sampling-temperature joke (#34); no star-rated review rows with a photo on the far right (#41); no magnifying-window gallery (#30); no giant word "sustainability" (#39); no particle ending; no confession or "if we can sell…" ending (#16, #17, #51).
- **Banned words** (ORYZO's punchlines, R§7.6): wearable, circular, encryption, antislip, grip, thermal, runs on, always on, drop-tested, legacy support, non-existent product. Also our own clichés: unleash, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionary.
- **Facts:** only `high` and `medium` items from `01-research.md`, plus `derived` numbers that the fact-check re-runs. `low` items are never used. Real people appear only as sourced historical facts or record holders, never as endorsers. Testimonials come from objects, animals and roles.
- **The deadpan formula** (teardown §7), in our own words: a 2–4 word claim, then about 30 words that take it literally, then a short, dry undercut. Never a wink, never an exclamation mark.

## Concept A: "Void of All Characters" (elegant and dry)

**The idea in one line.** aviva is launched as the purest foundation model ever made: no data, no outputs, no hallucinations. Its intelligence comes from experience, as John Locke said of the newborn mind in 1689, when he called it "white paper, void of all characters" (R§5.3, high). The experience in question is yours.

**Model name: aviva A4.** The version number is the paper size, and the model family is the A-series: aviva A5 is the "distilled" model, aviva A3 "has twice the context", and every one of them is exactly the same shape (R§1.1, high).

**Tagline: Pre-trained on nothing.**

**Central parody angle.** The reverence of foundation-model launches and Apple-style minimalism: "less is more" design sermons, hallucination rates, context windows, distillation, blind-test arenas and release notes, all applied to a product whose single feature is that there is nothing on it. "Void of all characters" works twice over: it's Locke's description of the mind, and it's an LLM with zero tokens of output.

**Tone.** Hushed, reverent and exact, like a keynote narrator who truly believes that blankness is the ultimate feature. Sentences of 3–8 words. Never a raised voice. The sheet is treated like a precious object, and every claim is literally true.

**Read in 5 seconds:** a perfect blank sheet floats down under the line "Every great product starts with a blank sheet of paper. Ours stopped there." **Payoff over 2 minutes:** the visitor writes on it, tears it in half, watches ink take over the page and votes in a blind test between two identical sheets. At the end, the sheet comes back carrying whatever the visitor drew.

### Five sample headlines (finished)
1. **Every great product starts with a blank sheet of paper. Ours stopped there.** (hero)
2. **Intelligence not included.** (the AI beat; alternative: "Intelligence sold separately.")
3. **Halve it. It's still aviva.** (√2 + the tear gimmick)
4. **Zero hallucinations. It has never said anything.** (claim card)
5. **Tested against the competition. The competition was also aviva.** (the Arena)

Extra lines in the same voice: "0.1 mm. From the side, it is barely here. That was the hard part." · "We weighed the weights." · "No glue. Just attraction." · "Answer: see above." · "Below A6, it's confetti."

### Three fake-precise metrics
- **Hallucination rate: 0.00 %** (n = 0 outputs).
- **Context window: 62,370 mm²** (210 × 297 mm, edge to edge; R§1.1 high, derived area).
- **Arena win rate against itself: 50.0 %** ("statistically indistinguishable from perfection").

### Hero moment
The blueprint loader (crop marks, the 210 and 297 dimension lines, and a counter running up the 297 line from 0 to 297 mm) resolves into the real sheet inside the crop marks. The marks retract. The sheet lifts and falls through the endless white studio, rocking like a falling leaf, which is how real paper falls. It comes to rest in front of a giant graphite **aviva** wordmark set dead centre. Wherever the sheet overlaps the letters, you can see them softly **through** it. Printers call that show-through, and it is the paper's opacity at work (R§1.4: one spec lists opacity 94 %, medium). Headline: "Every great product starts with a blank sheet of paper." One scroll later: "Ours stopped there." Small side copy: "aviva A4. Pre-trained on nothing." with a spec line: "210 × 297 mm · 80 g/m² · 0.1 mm".

### Ending moment
Back in the white studio, the sheet rises to face the visitor at hero size. **It carries whatever the visitor drew in the AI beat**, because aviva kept it. The line: **"Pre-trained on nothing. Fine-tuned on you."** If the visitor never drew anything, the sheet comes back blank and the line reads: **"Pre-trained on nothing. Still nothing. We respect that."** The sheet then folds itself into a paper plane and glides out of frame. Under it: "aviva was designed and built by RelentlessYunn" and a single button, **github.com/RelentlessYunn** (https://github.com/RelentlessYunn). Footer disclaimer below.

### The AI beat: "Intelligence not included." (the writing moment)
Pinned. The sheet tilts towards the visitor like a writing slope, and the cursor becomes a pencil. Body: "aviva A4 ships with no data, no knowledge and no opinions. In 1689, John Locke called this 'white paper, void of all characters'. We call it the base model. Pick up the pencil." The visitor writes or draws, and graphite strokes go into the sheet's ink layer with real tooth and smudge. A status line under the sheet responds with exaggerated patience: "aviva is thinking." → "aviva has thought about it." → **"Answer: see above."**
- **Undo:** "Turn the pencil round." The cursor flips to the eraser end, and erasing leaves a faint ghost of the stroke, as it does on real paper.
- **Regenerate:** a small button. The sheet crumples into a ball, rolls out of frame, and a fresh sheet floats down. Counter: "499 regenerations left in this ream" (a ream is 500 sheets, R§1.2 high). This parodies the endless regenerate button and the novelist's bin of crumpled drafts in one move. The site keeps the last drawing for the ending.

### The interactive gimmick (replaces flip encryption): "Distil it."
**What the visitor does:** drags along a dotted line across the middle of the sheet (or presses and holds "Distil" for keyboard and touch users). **What the sheet does:** it **tears** along the line, following the drag. The torn edge has a fibrous fringe that glows when back-lit. The two halves drift apart and settle side by side, and dimension lines re-measure each one: same 1 : √2 shape, half the area. Readout: "aviva A5 × 2. 2.49 g each. Capabilities: unchanged." A second tear gives "aviva A6 × 4". A third is refused with a small, final line: **"Below A6, it's confetti."**
**Why it's funny:** model distillation promises smaller models that keep their capabilities. Paper actually delivers: halve an A-series sheet and you get the same shape (R§1.1 high). These smaller models also keep exactly the same knowledge, which is none. **Why it isn't flip encryption:** no message, no hiding and no text on the object. The input is a gesture, and the result is a physical change you can measure. It's also our draggable scrub control (teardown checklist #33), and it is not an arc slider.

### Our signature transition: "The Bleed"
It's built on a property only paper has: **it drinks ink.** In the macro beat, the camera sits at fibre scale. A single drop of ink-blue lands, and it **wicks along the fibres**, spreading faster along the grain than across it. It keeps spreading past the edge of the sheet until the whole viewport is ink-blue. The page ground, the nav and the type flip to paper white on ink-blue. That is our global colour change (checklist #20), and it doesn't happen as a cross-fade: the paper absorbs it. The way back is just as paper-native: after two sections on ink, **a fresh white sheet** (a DOM block with a crisp paper edge and a soft shadow) slides up over the ink and becomes the white ground again (checklist #21).
It isn't a frame expanding to full-bleed (#32), it isn't a vector-ring match-cut (#20), and it isn't ORYZO's cross-fade with a leaf gobo (#39).

### Why it's funny
It applies the most hyped product category on earth to a product whose only feature is absence, and every line is technically true: zero hallucinations, a 62,370 mm² context window, distillation that keeps the capabilities (none). The craft is the punchline, as it was for ORYZO. We spend gorgeous light and real physics on seven renders of the same empty output. Then the ending turns the joke back on the visitor: the only training data aviva ever got was their doodle.

### Why it suits paper
Being blank is the one claim nothing but paper can make. Tearing an A-series sheet in half and keeping its shape is unique to this exact object. The rest is paper too: ink wicking through fibres, show-through, a falling-leaf descent and fibres held together by hydrogen bonds.

### Risks
- **"Nothing" could wear thin over 2 minutes.** Mitigation: an interaction every 8–10 vh (write, tear, vote), new facts in every beat, and the payoff of the doodle returning at the end.
- **The Apple-minimal register is a well-worn parody style.** The writing must be exact and specific (numbers, dates, mm), never vague.
- **Seven blank sheets could look like a lazy gallery.** Each render needs its own light setup; the visual-designer must make each card individually beautiful.
- **Closeness to ORYZO:** the closest match is the deadpan premium tone, which is a KEEP. The fly-through type (#29) is kept as a technique without the bloom or the comma-eyebrow formula. The tier selector changes size, not count, so it can't be confused with #47. Overall similarity risk: **low**.
- **Build:** the Bleed uses the closed-form, scroll-scrubbable "ink front" shader proven in R-tech §2.13 (not the ping-pong simulation, which is too slow). It runs at full quality inside the microscope view, then at half resolution as the ink floods the viewport, and finishes as a cheap ragged-edge screen wipe once the ink covers the screen. The tear uses the proven two-copy technique with a per-fragment jagged discard and a fibrous white fringe (R-tech §2.5); the A6 step needs 4 copies, drawn only while tearing. The Regenerate crumple is fine in motion (R-tech §2.6), and the ball rolls out of frame before anyone can study it at rest. The Arena and the drying line use instanced copies of the sheet at lower resolution. Overall effort: **medium**.

## Concept B: "It Can Be Anything" (bold and absurd)

**The idea in one line.** aviva is launched as the first truly general-purpose model: one sheet that becomes a plane, a fan, a boat or a crane. The launch has all the theatre of an AGI announcement: agent demos, benchmark charts, an app store, and a scaling roadmap that reaches the Moon.

**Model name: aviva Omni.** These days every frontier model claims it can do everything. A sheet of paper can be folded into almost anything, so this is the same claim, except true. It's a single word: no number and no checkpoint suffix.

**Tagline: It can be anything. Mostly a plane.**

**Central parody angle.** AGI and the agent gold rush, at full volume: "it can do anything", agentic demos where the agent goes off and does the job, tool use, benchmark bar charts, scaling-law extrapolations, live demos on stage, "one more thing" and app stores. The mapping:
- general intelligence = origami (one sheet, any shape; folding can even trisect an angle);
- agentic = the plane flies off on its own;
- scaling = every fold doubles the thickness;
- benchmarks = real paper records;
- app store = origami models;
- training = creases.

**Tone.** A loud, kinetic, maximal keynote, in the stage voice of a founder who has seen the future and found that it is A4. Huge numbers, huge type, fast choreography and sincere superlatives. It is still deadpan: no exclamation marks and no winks. The absurdity is the gap between the scale of each claim and the product's 4.99 g, and every claim stays literally true.

**Read in 5 seconds:** in mid-air, the sheet folds itself into a plane, loops round the giant wordmark and lands flat: "It can be anything." … "Mostly a plane." **Payoff over 2 minutes:** you give it a task and it flies away with it. You fold it towards the Moon and hit a wall at six. You install origami "apps". By the end, the sheet is covered in every crease it has learned.

### Five sample headlines (finished)
1. **It can be anything. Mostly a plane.** (hero)
2. **Give it a task. Watch it leave.** (the AI beat)
3. **Our roadmap reaches the Moon. Our sheet reaches 6.4 mm.** (the scaling gimmick; R§3.1 derived)
4. **One developer. 50,000 apps. A few hundred released.** (the aviva Store; Akira Yoshizawa, R§5.1 high)
5. **It can trisect an angle. Your compass can't.** (reasoning; R§5.1 high)

Extra lines in the same voice: "Four parameters. All active." · "The only demo that can't crash. It can only crease." · "Eligible for every record on this chart." · "One more sheet." · "Fully trained."

### Three fake-precise metrics
- **Parameters: 4 total, 4 active.** These are the corners. It parodies the "active parameters" in mixture-of-experts specs (R§10 #2–3).
- **Scaling efficiency: ×2 thickness per fold, wall at fold 6.** That's one A4 sheet folded in one direction: 0.1 mm × 2⁶ = 6.4 mm (R§3.1 derived; the fact-check must re-run it).
- **Shapes requested by users: plane, 97.3 %.** The figure is printed with the visible hedge "(internal survey, n = 3, all of us)", so nobody can read the joke number as a fact.

### Hero moment
The loader draws the A4 outline with crop marks and dimension lines. ### The interactive gimmick (replaces flip encryption): "Scale it."

### Our signature transition: "The Unfold"
It's built on a property only paper has: **a single sheet can take any shape and come back flat, with the creases still in it.** Each major section ends with the current form opening back into the flat sheet, which faces the camera at the centre of the screen. The next section folds its form out of that flat moment, so the hand-over always happens on the flat frame and the product never leaves its place on screen. Every unfold leaves its pattern behind as faint mountain and valley lines. They build up across the page (plane → fan pleats → halving grid → Miura parallelograms → the installed app) until the ending's "Fully trained." crease map.
This is not a frame expanding (#32), not a ring match-cut (#20), and not a dissolve. The sheet's own history is the transition.
**Build note:** the Unfold only uses forms we can fold procedurally (plane, fan, halvings, Miura, curls; R-tech §2.2, §2.7). The crane, boat and fortune teller are hand-modelled meshes and appear only on the gallery plinths. ### Why it's funny
"It can do anything" is the most exhausted claim in AI. A sheet of paper is the one product for which it is honestly true, and yet everybody just makes a plane. Every hype ritual gets a physical answer:
- the agent literally flies off with your task;
- the scaling curve hits a wall you trigger with your own finger;
- the benchmark chart is real world records the product is "eligible" for;
- the app store's star developer made 50,000 apps and released a few hundred.
The big, confident voice makes every tiny true number funnier.

### Why it suits paper
For a single sheet, folding is its only "general purpose", and its real properties map neatly onto AI's big words: creases as memory and training, flight as autonomy, thickness doubling as scaling. The records behind them are hard numbers.

## Concept C: "Too Dangerous to Release" (unexpected)

**The idea in one line.** aviva is launched by a frightened, safety-first lab that treats one sheet of paper as the most dangerous model it has ever built. Every feature is disclosed as a risk, every section is an evaluation, and the whole launch is a careful, staged release of something humanity has had for over two thousand years.

**Model name: aviva Preview.** "Research preview" is how a nervous lab ships something. This one has been in preview since at least 179 BCE (Fangmatan, R§2 high).

**Tagline: Too capable to keep in a drawer.**

**Central parody angle.** AI-safety theatre and doom as marketing: system cards, red-teaming, dangerous-capability evaluations, responsible scaling policies, kill switches, model welfare, staged rollouts, and the press tour where the founder says they're frightened of what they've built. Every safety idea has an exact, true paper equivalent:
- jailbreak = writing on it;
- self-replication = the photocopier;
- autonomous flight = the plane;
- evasion = 0.1 mm;
- harm = the paper cut;
- kill switch = crumpling;
- alignment = text alignment;

- decommissioning = recycling (it comes back).
**The target is the theatre, never safety work itself.** The lab is sincere, careful and kind; the joke is that its object is harmless, apart from the edges.

It's unexpected because everyone parodies AI launches as hype, and this one parodies them as dread.

A puncture opens at the centre and the sheet tears outwards along six to eight jagged radial lines. Each petal curls back towards the camera, its torn edge glowing white with raw fibres, and the next scene is revealed through the hole. While it happens, the status label flips to **"BREACH"** in the accent at a large size: this is the UI's one rule-break.
It's radial, its petals curl, and it reveals a 3D scene, so it is not ORYZO's bark split, where two halves swing away like doors to reveal a giant word (#38). It is not a frame expanding (#32) or a ring match-cut (#20) either.

Each wedge discards everything outside itself and curls back on its own hinge fold (R-tech §2.4). Six draws of the sheet happen only during the 1.5 vh of each breach; phones get four petals on the lower-resolution sheet.

### Why it's funny
It's the solemnity. The industry's most self-serious genre, the safety disclosure, gets applied to the least dangerous object in the building, except for its edges. Those really do hurt, and that is the one finding the lab handles calmly. Every safety idea lands on a real, true paper fact:
- it cannot refuse what you write;
- it copies itself, given a photocopier;
- its kill switch doesn't quite stay off;
- alignment has four settings;
- decommissioning only recycles it, five to seven times.

### Why it suits paper
A sheet really can't refuse what's written on it. It really slips under doors, really cuts and really comes back from recycling. Every fear is true; only the scale is absurd.

### Risks
- **Tone.** Dread could tip into sour or preachy, or read as mocking safety work itself. Mitigation: the lab is sincere and gentle, and the target is the theatre. We never name real labs or real models, and never imply that real risks are silly.
- **It moves furthest from ORYZO's Apple-style hype register**, which is the KEEP tone. The craft and voice must stay deadpan premium, never office satire.
- **5-second clarity** depends on the hero line plus the "CONTAINED" label. It needs testing on someone who has never seen it.
- **Build:** the Breach is the most complex transition (six wedge copies), so it's used only twice. The kill-switch ball is fine at mid distance, but a close-up would need the offline bake (R-tech §2.6). The shred needs a sheet mesh with cut seams so the strips fall separately. Transmitted light, the tear, the pencil, the halvings and the instanced ream are proven. Overall effort: **medium–high**.
- **The access tiers change the sheet's marks, not its count, size or form.** Checklist #34 may want a physical change. The fallback is A's A-size selector.
- **Closeness to ORYZO:**
  - the incident reports avoid the review assembly (#41): no stars, counts or photos;
  - the Breach is radial and reveals a scene, not a word (#38);
  - transmitted light is physically lit and monochrome (#8).
  - Overall similarity risk: **low**.

### Recommendation: Concept A, "Void of All Characters" (the lead decides)

1. **It has the best 5-second hook.** "Every great product starts with a blank sheet of paper. Ours stopped there." works on someone who has never heard of ORYZO.
2. **It is the purest version of the KEEP.** It has ORYZO's deadpan premium launch, product-centred storytelling and restraint, with every joke, interaction, transition and character new.
3. **Every interaction is something only this exact object can do.** It is blank. Torn in half, it keeps its shape. It drinks ink. It shows type through itself. It falls like a leaf.
4. **It puts the budget where the mission says the hero is: one sheet that has to look real.** It doesn't spread the effort across a dozen origami forms.
5. **It ends on the visitor.** Their own doodle comes back, "fine-tuned on you", which nobody else's ending does.

**The strongest alternative is C.** If the lead wants maximum distinctiveness over maximum fidelity, choose C.
