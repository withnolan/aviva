# Fact-check of `work/04-creative-brief.md` (Parts 3, 4, 5 and 6)

Reviewer: web-researcher. Date: 2026-10-02. Scope: every real-world claim in the site copy (Part 3), the research paper (Part 4), the visual-direction notes (Part 5) and the delight list (Part 6). Obvious parody claims (hallucination rate 0.00 %, Arena 50.0 %, "14 years in service, 2,031 jams", and so on) are not flagged unless a reader could take them for a real fact.

## How this was checked, and its limits

- About 40 WebSearch queries in this pass, plus the earlier sources in `work/01-research.md`. **WebFetch was blocked for every host I tried** (wikipedia, afnor, historyofinformation, infostory, damninteresting, researchgate, sciencedirect, aheckofa, expexinc, csmonitor). I did not try to get round any block. So all evidence is search-result summaries, and I say so in the confidence tags.
- Tags: **high** = two or more agreeing sources, or the primary document quoted; **med** = one reputable summary or agreeing secondary sources; **derived** = my arithmetic (re-run by script-style hand calculation, shown); **low** = do not publish.
- Verdicts: **correct**, **wrong**, **can't verify**, **needs rewording** (true in spirit but stronger, vaguer or more specific than the source supports).
- `R§` refers to `work/01-research.md`. New facts from this pass are appended there as §13.10 onwards.

## 3. Missing reference details (brief Part 8), with sources and confidence

1. **Gallivan's booklet (2002).** *How to Fold Paper in Half Twelve Times: An "Impossible Challenge" Solved and Explained.* Pomona, California: Historical Society of Pomona Valley, 2002; a pamphlet of about 40 pages. **Confidence: high for title and publisher** (several independent search summaries agree: Live Science, Mental Floss, Damn Interesting, a Guinness-related page quoting it); page count is from one summary (med). The record itself: Guinness lists 12 folds, 27 January 2002, with 4,000 ft (1,219 m) of tissue paper, and gives both formulas.
2. **Business Week 1975.** "The Office of the Future." *Business Week*, issue of **30 June 1975**. Contains the Vincent E. Giuliano (Arthur D. Little) quote that paper use for records "should be declining by 1980, and by 1990, most record-handling will be electronic", and George Pake's (Xerox PARC) 1995 office. **Confidence: title high; date med-high (several search summaries give "June 30, 1975"; I could not open the scan); page range (commonly cited 48–70) low, do not print it.**
3. **Priestley (1770).** *A Familiar Introduction to the Theory and Practice of Perspective.* London: printed for J. Johnson and J. Payne, 1770 (dedication dated Leeds, 20 March 1770). The passage is a footnote after the preface, dated 15 April 1770 in secondary sources: "I have seen a substance excellently adapted to the purpose of wiping from paper the mark of black-lead pencil ... It is sold by Mr. Nairne, Mathematical Instrument-Maker, opposite the Royal Exchange." **Confidence: high for title, year and the quoted phrase; med for publisher (library catalogue summaries); the claim that he "named" it "rubber" is not supported (see row 71).**
4. **ISO 216.** *ISO 216:2007, Writing paper and certain classes of printed matter — Trimmed sizes — A and B series, and indication of machine direction.* Published 29 Aug 2007 (some listings say 28 Aug), replaces *ISO 216:1975* (first published May 1975, same title without "and indication of machine direction"); **confirmed (still current) on 10 Sept 2021**. **Confidence: high** (AFNOR, ANSI, CCN, EVS and other standards-store listings agree). The **edition number** is reported inconsistently (one source "Edition 2", ISO's own page probably "Edition 3"); **do not print an edition number**; cite by year. The 1975 "first edition" is correct for the *ISO 216* number; I did not verify whether an earlier ISO Recommendation (ISO/R 216, 1961) existed, so avoid "first ever ISO paper standard".
5. **C5 check.** **Verified.** C5 = 162 × 229 mm (ISO 269). It takes A5 flat or A4 folded once (A4 folded once = 148.5 × 210 mm). A3 folded twice = 148.5 × 210 mm (A5 size, four layers), so it also fits; A3 is **derived** (the sources list A5 and A4 explicitly). **Confidence: high for C5 and A5/A4; derived for A3.**

Also confirmed in this pass: the **Dias & Arroja** DOI (10.1016/j.jclepro.2011.11.005) and the three numbers (4.64 / 4.74 / 4.29 g CO₂ eq per A4 sheet, cradle-to-customer; high); the **EPRC** 2024 press release (July 2025; 75.1 %; high); **Locke's** publication in December 1689 with a 1690 title page (high).
